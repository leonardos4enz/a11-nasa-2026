# Fire season, category rules and burn regulations

## How the fire season is defined
The calendar builds a typical year from the median of each calendar month across 2003-2025 (2001-2002 rest on Terra alone, so they are shown but not used for the typical year). The fire season runs from
the month where 10% of the typical yearly hotspots have accumulated to the month where 90% have, so it holds about 80%
of the activity. The peak is the month with the highest typical count. The Season tab also compares the peak month in
2001-2010 with 2016-2025 to show whether the season has shifted.

## Record high and streak rules
A month is "Record high" when it beats the same month of every other year by at least 5 hotspots, so a tiny margin
does not count as a record. The Overview warns "Above normal for N months in a row" when two or more consecutive
months are Above normal, Much above normal or Record high.

## What the Burn tab calculates
In the Burn tab the user selects the months when burning would be allowed. The page shows what share of the area's
hotspots fall in those months in an average year (2003-2025), how activity in those months changed from 2001-2010 to
2016-2025, and a warning for selected months that hold 15% or more of the yearly activity, suggesting burns be
prohibited then.

## Burn regulation in Mexico (NOM-015)
In Mexico, NOM-015-SEMARNAT-AGRICULTURA-2023, section 5, asks every municipal government to set each year the dates
when agricultural and forest burns are allowed. Until now no public tool showed municipalities 25 years of fire
history for their own territory; Ember Atlas gives them the typical season, the peak and how it changed, for any
municipality or drawn area.

## Burn regulation in the United States
In the United States there is no single national burn calendar: burn seasons, permits and burn bans are set by each
state's forestry agency and by local fire authorities. For US areas the Burn tab shows the same activity analysis
without citing a federal rule.
